Faster image loading

Notebook: ImageNet-Unify.ipynb. Five small edits, then four cells to run again. The model and its weights are not touched. These are the same edits the 3090 timing runs used.

Measured on the 3090 (baseline train step, batch 64)

LoaderPer step, shards in memoryPer step, shards read from the disk
As in the notebook (2 workers)about 99 msabout 99 ms, with stalls
These edits (4 workers, one disk reader, reduced-size decode)31 ms, the GPU's own speedabout 150 ms

Over a 4,000-step run the edits averaged 79 ms per step against about 99 ms: 1.25 times faster. The image decoding is no longer the limit. Reading 112 KiB per image from the dataset disk is.

Good to know

  • The speed comes in bursts. While the shards are in the PC's memory cache, steps run at the GPU's speed. When the loader has to read new shards from the dataset disk, steps slow to about 150 ms until it catches up. A long run reads far more than fits in memory.
  • The reduced-size decode changes the pixels a little. On 256 real images: mean difference 0.007 on the 0 to 1 scale, 99% of values within 0.066, largest 0.37 at sharp edges. The image mean does not shift. Leave out step 2 to keep the pixels exactly as before. Full decode with 6 workers measured about 1,100 images per second on the 3090, which is 58 ms per step.
  • Why one disk reader at a time. In a direct test the dataset disk read 154 MiB/s as one stream, and 9 to 32 MiB/s in total with 2 to 6 streams at once.
  • Evaluations before and after are not on the same images, if you use the reduced-size decode: get_split_loss keeps its first 20 batches. To compare cleanly, run get_eval_batches.cache.clear() and eval_logs.clear() after the edits.
  • What would remove the disk limit: a pre-resized copy of the dataset, or the shards on a faster disk. Not part of these edits.
